A Classroom Exercise in Survival Analysis

The Life Ledger

Allocate ten points across five traits, then live out a simulated life. Three more traits — happiness, risk tendency, and vitality — emerge on their own from your choices, and quietly decide how long you live.

Before you begin, choose the setting your life will play out in. Same rules, same traits — but very different starting conditions. Try one, then come back and try the other, and compare what happened.

Standard Mode

A Well-Resourced Setting

Represents a well-resourced ("1st-world") setting: strong healthcare access, reliable nutrition, stable schooling, and a real social safety net. Traits grow faster and cap out much higher. Dying before 60 is designed to be extremely rare here — under a 1-in-1000 chance for a typically-played character — and setbacks, when they happen, are easier to recover from.

Hard Mode

A Low-Resource Setting

Represents a lower-income setting with limited access to healthcare, nutrition, education, and steady work. Traits grow more slowly and cap out lower. Setbacks — illness, injury, missed opportunities — land harder and more often, and there's less cushion to absorb them. Dying young is still uncommon, but far less rare than in Standard Mode, and becomes much more likely if Health and Fitness are neglected.

Neither mode is modelled on any one specific place or people — they're deliberately contrasting difficulty settings, built to make a simple point: the length and shape of a life isn't just a matter of individual choices. It's also a matter of the conditions those choices are made in.
