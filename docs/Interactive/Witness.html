The Witness Test

Eyewitness Memory Overconfidence Calibration Uncertainty

About

This app puts students in the position of an eyewitness. They watch a short 15-second animated crime scene, then answer 10 multiple-choice questions about what they saw — rating their confidence alongside each answer. The gap between confidence and accuracy is the whole point: students are almost always more sure of themselves than their actual scores justify, mirroring a well-documented finding in eyewitness testimony research. This is a great entry point for discussing calibration (does 80% confidence really mean 80% correct?), the reliability of eyewitness evidence in legal contexts, and how overconfidence can quietly distort real-world decision-making even when someone feels certain.

General questions

  • Before seeing your score, how confident were you overall that you’d remembered the scene accurately? Did your actual score match that feeling?
  • Were there any specific questions where you felt very confident but got the answer wrong? What might explain that gap?
  • Does higher confidence always mean higher accuracy? Based on your own results, what would you say the relationship actually looks like?
  • If a real eyewitness tells police “I’m 100% sure that’s the person I saw,” should that statement alone be treated as strong evidence? Why or why not?
  • What factors do you think affect how well someone remembers a brief, stressful event — and were any of those factors present (or missing) in this 15-second clip?
  • As a class, compare everyone’s confidence-vs-accuracy patterns. Does everyone show the same overconfidence pattern, or do some people seem better calibrated than others?
  • Courts often rely heavily on eyewitness testimony, sometimes as the primary piece of evidence in a case. Based on this activity, what changes (if any) do you think should be made to how eyewitness evidence is used or presented in trials?
  • Outside of courtrooms, where else in life might overconfidence in your own memory or judgment cause problems, even when you feel completely certain?
